01 — Research

What Counts as a Usable Performance?

My research begins in the studio, at the point where optical capture of the hands stops being usable. Marker streams on small, frequently occluded joints drift, pop, freeze and swap between fingers. Automated cleanup can repair these streams to a numerical standard, yet animators still routinely discard the result: the numbers improve, but the gesture no longer reads as intentional human movement.

I treat these post-IK anomalies not merely as data defects but as breakdowns in expressive intent — moments when a character's gesture stops communicating and the audience loses access to the performance. The project maps where anomalies originate across the capture-to-engine pipeline and asks who decides that a repaired motion is good enough to ship.

The work joins making with documentation. I produce and analyse edited sequences, then formulate the criteria animators actually apply — narrative legibility of gesture, character fit, and editability — alongside measured data accuracy, testing them with practitioners through blind rating and reported inter-rater reliability. The aim is a practice-led framework that turns informal frame-by-frame judgment into a reproducible method.

Questions guiding the work

  • Where in the capture-to-engine pipeline do the anomalies that matter to an audience originate?
  • Do practitioner judgments of a repaired performance align with the error metrics used to repair it?
  • Can editability — a clip's tolerance to further animator adjustment — become a reliable criterion for evaluation?
02 — Creative & Technical Practice

Selected Work

Four short pieces, each with the problem, my role, and what the work demonstrates. Together they show the making side of the research: capture pipelines, image-making, and editing as decision-making.

2026 · Applied Research · Motion Capture

Motion-Capture Pipeline Showcase

Role: pipeline design, capture operation, data repair, retargeting, in-engine integration

A demonstration of the motion-capture programme I established at the university: performer marker placement, capture, data repair, T-pose calibration, retargeting and integration in Unreal. The showcase is both a teaching resource and the practical ground of my research into where hand data fails.

Original language: Mandarin (voice-over and on-screen titles). Moving image and pipeline work are my own.

2025 · Competition Work · Motion Capture & Real-Time Rendering

The Bracelet (星镯)

Role: screenwriter and narrative designer; story-line editing; opening and closing title sequences; overall production supervision

A competition entry in which I authored the script and narrative structure, edited the story line, produced the opening and closing title sequences, and supervised the overall production. The piece is a practical test of the capture-to-engine chain I research: how a performance survives retargeting and in-engine integration, and where its expressive detail is lost or preserved on screen.

Competition work; motion capture and real-time rendering. Visuals presented with minimal English context as the work is largely nonverbal.

2024 · Creative Practice · Generative Image-Making & Editing

The Journey Home (归途)

Role: image generation, editing, music, narrative structure

A short film built from generated images that I edited and scored into a continuous narrative. My contribution is the selection, sequencing, rhythm and sound — turning generated material into a piece that reads as a coherent visual story. Selected for a national digital-arts competition.

Original language: Mandarin (on-screen titles and voice-over). Image generation, editing and scoring are my own work.

2023 · Editing Practice · Short Form

Emotional Short — Editing Study

Role: editor — shot selection, pacing, sound

A short editing study exploring how rhythm and sound shape emotional reading without dialogue. Made as a teaching demonstration in post-production; the decisions here — what to keep, what to cut, how long a gesture must hold to register — inform the editability question in my research.

Original language: Mandarin (on-screen titles). Editing and sound are my own work.

Toolset

Motion capture: optical and inertial systems, Motive · Data repair and retargeting: MotionBuilder · In-engine animation: Unreal Engine · Editing and compositing: Premiere, After Effects · Generative image-making and sound assembly.

03 — Teaching

Assistant Lecturer · Yinchuan University of Science and Technology

I teach animation and visual effects in a studio-based curriculum, with an emphasis on students finishing work rather than only studying it. I initiated the university's motion-capture programme, defined its equipment and software specifications, and wrote the standardised pipeline documentation covering the full chain from marker placement and capture to repair, calibration, retargeting and in-engine integration.

Core courses taught

  • Film and Television Animation
  • Visual Effects
  • Digital Film and Television Technology

Teaching is not separate from the research: years of watching where cleaned-up hand data fails on screen form the empirical basis of the questions I ask.
